DrJekyll

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 24, 2026

The short version DrJekyll does not collect, store, or send your health information to us. Your lab results stay on your device. The app organizes your lab results; it does not diagnose anything or give medical advice. The one time information leaves your device is when you choose to paste a prompt into Claude yourself.

What DrJekyll Does

DrJekyll is a personal lab result monitoring app for cancer patients. It reads lab results from Apple Health on your iPhone, displays them as trend charts over time, and helps you prepare better questions for your care team. The app is published by DRJEKYLLAPPS LLC, a New York limited liability company ("we" or "us"). Earlier versions were called "Dr. Jekyll," and you may still see that name in the app while we complete the change.

DrJekyll connects the dots between the lab data already in your Apple Health records and a clearer picture of your trends over time. It does not diagnose anything, does not give medical advice, and does not generate clinical recommendations of its own.

No Medical Advice

DrJekyll is an organizational tool, not a medical one. Nothing displayed in the app, and nothing generated through the optional Ask Claude feature described below, is medical advice, a diagnosis, or a treatment recommendation.

Lab data shown in the app reflects whatever has synced to Apple Health from your providers' records, and may be incomplete, delayed, or affected by sync errors. Reference ranges shown are either the ranges your lab supplied with the result or general defaults, and may differ from the ranges your doctor uses. Always talk to your oncologist or care team about what your results mean and before making any decision based on anything you see in this app.

What We Collect

Through the app: nothing. DrJekyll has no backend server, database, account system, analytics, crash-reporting service, or advertising. No company server touches your health data.

The only information the app stores on your device is your preferences: which lab tests you have chosen to monitor and your date range settings. These are stored locally using Apple's SwiftData framework and stay on your device.

Outside the app, limited information may reach us in these ways:

  • Beta feedback form. If you are a TestFlight tester and choose to submit our feedback form (hosted by Google Forms), we receive only what you type into it. Please do not enter lab values or other personal health details. Google processes form responses under its own privacy policy.
  • Email. If you email us, we receive your email address and your message, and we use them only to respond.
  • Our website. This page is hosted on GitHub Pages and loads fonts from Google Fonts. Like most web hosts and font services, they may receive technical information such as your IP address when you visit. We do not run analytics or advertising trackers on this page.
  • Apple. Apple, not us, operates TestFlight and the App Store. If you choose to share TestFlight feedback or crash reports with developers, or when you download the app, Apple may give us limited information as described in Apple's terms.

We do not sell your personal information and do not share it for advertising.

Your Health Data

DrJekyll reads lab results from Apple Health using Apple's HealthKit framework. This requires your explicit permission, which you grant through Apple's standard health authorization prompt.

  • DrJekyll reads the specific lab result types that you choose to monitor.
  • DrJekyll never writes to Apple Health.
  • DrJekyll never stores your lab values on disk. Results are loaded into memory when you view them and discarded when you leave the screen.
  • DrJekyll never transmits your health data to us or to any server we control. No lab values, dates or identifiers leave your device through DrJekyll, except as described under Ask Claude, where you decide whether to share.

You can revoke DrJekyll's access to Apple Health at any time:

Settings → Privacy & Security → Health → DrJekyll

Ask Claude Feature (Optional)

DrJekyll includes a feature that helps you build a prompt to bring into a conversation with Claude, a separate AI product made by Anthropic, PBC. Before you can use it, the app shows a consent screen explaining what information will be included and who receives it. This feature:

  • Builds a text prompt on your device from the lab results you select (test names, recent values, dates, units, and reference ranges)
  • Shows you what will be included before anything happens, and asks you to confirm that you understand this data is about to leave the app
  • Copies the prompt to your clipboard only when you choose to proceed
  • Opens the Claude app or website, where you paste the prompt yourself

DrJekyll itself never sends any data to Claude or Anthropic. The prompt leaves your device only when you paste and send it in the Claude app, and from that point Anthropic's privacy practices apply, not ours. We cannot see, retrieve, or delete what you share with Claude. Anthropic's privacy policy is at anthropic.com/legal/privacy.

Any response you receive from Claude, including trend analysis, plain-language explanations, or suggested questions for your doctor, is generated by Anthropic's product. We do not review or curate it, and it is not medical advice.

You can turn this feature off at any time in Health Connection settings.

Face ID

DrJekyll uses Face ID (or your device passcode as a fallback) to prevent others from viewing your health data if they pick up your phone. Face ID is handled entirely by Apple's LocalAuthentication framework. DrJekyll never sees or stores your biometric data.

Security

Because your lab results stay on your device, their protection depends mainly on your device settings. Use a passcode, keep your iPhone updated, and be careful about who has access to your unlocked phone and your Apple Health data. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.

Your Choices

  • Turn off Ask Claude in Health Connection settings.
  • Revoke Apple Health access in the iOS Settings path above.
  • Delete the app to remove its stored preferences from your device.
  • Ask us about anything you submitted by email or through the feedback form, and we will delete it on request.

Demo Mode

DrJekyll includes a Demo Mode that displays sample lab data for a fictional Multiple Myeloma patient. Demo Mode does not use or display any of your real health data.

Beta Testing (TestFlight)

If you are using DrJekyll through Apple TestFlight, you are using pre-release software that may contain bugs or incomplete features. Use during this period is at your own risk, and the app should not be relied on as your sole source of health information.

International Users

DrJekyll may be used outside the United States. Because the app has no backend and does not transmit your health data to us, most data protection obligations that apply to companies storing data on servers do not arise the same way here. If you use the optional Ask Claude feature, your data is subject to Anthropic's own privacy practices at that point, regardless of where you are located.

Children's Privacy

DrJekyll is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from children.

Changes to This Policy

If this policy changes materially, we will update the date at the top of this page. Because the app itself collects no data, we expect changes to be minimal.
